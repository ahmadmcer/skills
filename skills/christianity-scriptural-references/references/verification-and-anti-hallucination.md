# Biblical Verification & Anti-Hallucination Guide

Read this guide when screening against common AI biblical hallucinations, handling textual variants,
or verifying disputed verses and folk aphorisms.

---

## 1. Why AI Models Hallucinate Scripture

LLMs frequently generate inaccurate biblical text because:

1. **Folk Aphorisms vs. Biblical Text**: Popular secular or cultural proverbs are regularly asserted as "Bible verses".
2. **Translation Blending**: Models frequently blend words from the KJV (e.g. archaic pronouns) with modern NIV or ESV sentences.
3. **Synoptic Conflation**: Merging Matthew's Beatitudes with Luke's Sermon on the Plain, or combining different details of the resurrection narratives without noting the distinct gospel authors.
4. **Verse Number Slippage**: Quoting text accurately but assigning the wrong chapter or verse number (e.g. confusing Romans 8:28 with Romans 12:2).

## 3. Major Textual Variants in the New Testament

When citing the following passages, modern scholarly integrity requires disclosing textual notes:

1. **The Longer Ending of Mark (Mark 16:9–20)**:
   - Absent from the earliest and most reliable Greek manuscripts (_Codex Sinaiticus_ and _Codex Vaticanus_), the earliest Latin, Syriac, and Sahidic manuscripts, and early Church Fathers (Eusebius, Jerome).
   - Most modern translations (ESV, NIV, NASB, NRSVue) print the passage in brackets with a footnote explaining the manuscript evidence.
2. **The Pericope Adulterae (The Woman Caught in Adultery, John 7:53–8:11)**:
   - Absent from the earliest Greek papyri ($\mathfrak{P}^{66}$, $\mathfrak{P}^{75}$) and early codices. First attested widely in the Western text tradition and Latin Vulgate.
   - Brackets and footnotes are standard in scholarly editions.
3. **The Comma Johanneum (1 John 5:7–8)**:
   - The famous explicit Trinitarian formula: _"For there are three that bear record in heaven, the Father, the Word, and the Holy Ghost: and these three are one."_
   - Found in the Latin Vulgate and Textus Receptus (KJV), but absent from all Greek manuscripts prior to the 10th century (and only in 8 late Greek manuscripts).
   - Modern translations based on NA28 omit this clause or relegate it to a critical footnote.
