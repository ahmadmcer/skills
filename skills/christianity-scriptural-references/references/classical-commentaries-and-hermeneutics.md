# Classical Commentaries & Hermeneutics Guide

Read this guide when conducting biblical exegesis, understanding the Historical-Grammatical method,
or referencing major commentary traditions across church history.

---

## 1. The Historical-Grammatical Hermeneutical Method

The standard methodology in orthodox biblical scholarship seeks to understand the author's
original intended meaning within its historical and literary context:

```
┌────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│         THE HISTORICAL-GRAMMATICAL HERMENEUTIC         │
├─────────────────┬──────────────────┬───────────────────┤
│ 1. Literary     │ 2. Historical &  │ 3. Grammatical &  │
│ Genre Context   │ Cultural Setting │ Linguistic Syntax │
├─────────────────┼──────────────────┼───────────────────┤
│ Identify genre  │ Who wrote it?    │ Study original    │
│ (Poetry, Law,   │ To whom? What    │ Hebrew/Greek word │
│ Epistles, etc.) │ cultural issue?  │ definitions & root│
└─────────────────┴──────────────────┴───────────────────┘
```

### The Rule of Literary Genre
- **Historical Narrative (Genesis, Samuel, Acts)**: Describes what *did* happen, not necessarily what *should* happen (descriptive vs. prescriptive).
- **Poetry & Wisdom (Psalms, Proverbs, Ecclesiastes)**: Utilizes Hebrew parallelism, metaphor, hyperbole, and figurative language. Proverbs are general wisdom principles, not absolute guarantees.
- **Epistles (Romans, Corinthians)**: Occasional pastoral letters addressing specific theological controversies or ethical pastoral concerns.
- **Apocalyptic (Daniel, Revelation)**: Employs rich cosmic symbolism, numerical structures, and dramatic visions to comfort persecuted believers.

## 2. Major Historical Commentary Traditions

### 1. The Early Church Fathers (*Patristics*, 100–600 CE)
- **St. Augustine of Hippo (354–430 CE)**: *On Christian Doctrine (De Doctrina Christiana)*, *Confessions*, *Expositions on the Psalms*. Foundational for Western theology on grace, original sin, and the supremacy of love as the hermeneutical key.
- **St. John Chrysostom (c. 347–407 CE)**: *Homilies on Matthew, John, and the Pauline Epistles*. The "Golden-Mouthed" preacher of Antioch; champion of literal and pastoral exposition.
- **St. Thomas Aquinas (1225–1274 CE)**: *Catena Aurea* (The Golden Chain: an exhaustive verse-by-verse patristic commentary on the four Gospels) and *Summa Theologiae*.

### 2. The Reformation & Post-Reformation (16th–17th Centuries)
- **John Calvin (1509–1564 CE)**: Meticulous commentaries on almost every biblical book. Pioneer of clear, lucid, grammatical exposition (*brevitas et facilitas*).
- **Martin Luther (1483–1546 CE)**: *Commentary on Galatians* and *Romans*. Emphasizes the distinction between Law and Gospel and Justification by Faith alone (*Sola Fide*).

### 3. Classic Pastoral & Expository Compendiums (18th–19th Centuries)
- **Matthew Henry (1662–1714 CE)**: *Exposition of the Old and New Testaments*. Celebrated for deep pastoral warmth, practical devotion, memorable proverbs, and spiritual insight.
- **Charles Haddon Spurgeon (1834–1892 CE)**: *The Treasury of David* (an exhaustive multi-volume masterpiece on the Psalms).

### 4. Modern Critical & Scholarly Commentary Series
- **NICNT / NICOT** (New International Commentary on the New/Old Testament): Rigorous, evangelical scholarship engaging original languages.
- **WBC** (Word Biblical Commentary): In-depth linguistic, textual, and form-critical commentary for scholars.
- **Pillar New Testament Commentary**: Blends rigorous exegesis with contemporary theological reflection.
