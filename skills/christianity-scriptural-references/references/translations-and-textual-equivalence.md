# Bible Translations & Textual Equivalence Guide

Read this guide when choosing, evaluating, or comparing English Bible translations,
understanding translation philosophies, or explaining textual divergences.

---

## 1. The Spectrum of Translation Philosophies

No translation is an exact 1:1 replica of ancient languages. Translators operate along a
continuum between **Formal Equivalence** and **Dynamic Equivalence**:

```
Formal Equivalence                          Optimal Equivalence                     Dynamic Equivalence
(Word-for-Word)                             (Balance / Mediating)                  (Thought-for-Thought)
      ◄──────────────────────────────────────────────┼──────────────────────────────────────────────►
  NASB95   ESV     KJV/NKJV      NRSVue             CSB                   NIV           NLT       The Message
(Most literal)                                                                        (Most readable/paraphrase)
```

## 3. Notable Textual Comparison Example

Comparing Romans 3:25 across translation philosophies:

- **ESV (Formal)**:
  > _"whom God put forward as a propitiation by his blood, to be received by faith."_
  > (Retains technical theological term _propitiation_ / ἱλαστήριον - appeasing wrath and expiating sin).
- **NIV (Dynamic)**:
  > _"God presented Christ as a sacrifice of atonement, through the shedding of his blood—to be received by faith."_
  > (Renders _propitiation_ as _"sacrifice of atonement"_ for broader communicative clarity).
- **KJV (Historical)**:
  > _"Whom God hath set forth to be a propitiation through faith in his blood"_
