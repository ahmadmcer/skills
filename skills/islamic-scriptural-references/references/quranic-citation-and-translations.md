# Quranic Citation & Translations Guide

Read this guide when citing verses from the Holy Qur'an, selecting English translations,
handling Uthmani script orthography, or formatting chapter and verse references.

---

## 1. Quranic Structure & Canonical Notation

The Holy Qur'an consists of 114 Surahs (chapters) and 6,236 Ayahs (verses) according to the
standard Kufic counting system (*'Adad al-Kufi*).

### Standard Citation Syntax
- **In-Text Brief**: `[Quran Surah:Ayah]` &rarr; `[Quran 2:255]`
- **Formal Title**: `Surah [Name] ([Number]:[Verse])` &rarr; `Surah Al-Baqarah (2:255)`
- **Verse Range**: `[Quran 18:10-12]`
- **Academic Footnote**: The Qur'an. Translated by Saheeh International. [2:255].

### Makki vs. Madani Surahs
- **Makki (Meccan)**: Revealed prior to the Hijrah (migration to Medina). Characterized by concise verses, creed (*'aqidah*), monotheism (*tawhid*), eschatology (Day of Judgment), stories of earlier Prophets, and rhetorical power.
- **Madani (Medinan)**: Revealed after the Hijrah. Characterized by detailed legal injunctions (*shariah*), governance, commercial transactions, family law, community relations, and hypocrites (*munafiqun*).

## 2. Comparative Analysis of Major English Translations

Because the Qur'an in English represents an interpretation (*Tarjamah*) rather than the divine
Arabic text, selecting the appropriate translation for the context is vital:

| Translation Edition | Translator & Background | Characteristics & Tone | Recommended Context |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Saheeh International** | Emily Assami, Mary Kennedy, Amatullah Bantley (1997) | Rigorous literal accuracy, close fidelity to traditional Sunnah orthodoxy, clear modern English. | Scholarly references, fiqh discussions, default academic baseline. |
| **The Clear Quran** | Dr. Mustafa Khattab (2016) | Highly readable, contemporary idiomatic English, thematic section headings, smooth flow. | General audiences, introductory readings, public articles. |
| **Oxford World's Classics** | M.A.S. Abdel Haleem (2004) | Academic prose, modern syntax, contextual commentary, natural English cadence without archaisms. | Academic research, comparative religion, university contexts. |
| **The Holy Qur'an: Text, Translation & Commentary** | Abdullah Yusuf Ali (1934) | Classical, poetic, Elizabethan phrasing (*"thee"*, *"thou"*), accompanied by extensive theological footnotes. | Literary study, historical reference, poetic citation. |
| **The Meaning of the Glorious Koran** | Marmaduke Pickthall (1930) | First English translation by a native English-speaking Muslim, archaic biblical register, literal. | Historical comparative analysis. |

### Rule of Translation Transparency
Always explicitly state which translation edition is being quoted:
> "Allah! There is no deity except Him, the Ever-Living, the Sustainer of [all] existence."
> — *[Quran 2:255, Saheeh International]*

## 3. The Uthmani Script (*Rasm al-'Uthmani*)

The standard Arabic orthography of the Qur'an is the Uthmani script, codified during the Caliphate
of Uthman ibn Affan (RA):
- Preserves classical phonetics and canonical recitation styles (*Qira'at*), predominantly the *Hafs 'an 'Asim* recitation.
- Includes specific diacritical marks:
  - *Sukun* variants (indicating clear pronunciation vs. assimilation/*idgham*).
  - *Madd* markers (denoting elongated vowels of 4 to 6 counts).
  - Stop/Pause marks (*Waqf*): *مـ* (mandatory stop), *لا* (do not stop), *ج* (permissible stop), *صلى* (preferred to continue), *قلى* (preferred to stop).
- Digital sources should adhere to verified standards like the **King Fahd Glorious Quran Printing Complex** or the **Tanzil Project**.
