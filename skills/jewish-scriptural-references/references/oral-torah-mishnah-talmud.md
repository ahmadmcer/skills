# The Oral Torah: Mishnah, Tosefta, Talmud, and Midrash

A comprehensive architectural reference to the corpus of the Oral Torah (*Torah Shebe'al Peh*), covering the Mishnah, Tosefta, Babylonian Talmud (*Bavli*), Jerusalem Talmud (*Yerushalmi*), and Midrash.

---

## 1. The Mishnah (מִשְׁנָה)

Redacted by **Rabbi Yehuda HaNasi** (Judah the Prince, known as *Rabbeinu HaKadosh*) around **200 CE** in the Galilee, the Mishnah is the foundational legal code of Rabbinic Judaism. Sages whose teachings appear in the Mishnah are known as **Tannaim** (repeaters/teachers).

The Mishnah is organized into **6 Orders** (*Shisha Sidrei Mishnah*, colloquially known by the acronym **Sha"s**), containing **63 Tractates** (*Masechtot*), divided into Chapters (*Perakim*) and Paragraphs (*Mishnayot*).

### The Six Orders (*Sedarim*)

```
┌────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│                        THE 6 ORDERS OF THE MISHNAH                     │
├────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ 1. Zera'im (Seeds)       → 11 Tractates (Agriculture & Blessings)      │
│ 2. Mo'ed (Festivals)     → 12 Tractates (Shabbat & Holy Days)          │
│ 3. Nashim (Women)        →  7 Tractates (Marriage, Divorce, Oaths)     │
│ 4. Nezikin (Damages)     → 10 Tractates (Civil/Criminal Law & Ethics)  │
│ 5. Kodashim (Holy Things)→ 11 Tractates (Temple Sacrifices & Dietary)  │
│ 6. Tahorot (Purities)    → 12 Tractates (Ritual Purity & Defilement)   │
└────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

#### Citation Format
- Academic standard (SBL): `m. [Tractate] [Chapter]:[Mishnah]`
- Examples:
  - `m. Ber. 1:1` (Mishnah Berakhot chapter 1, mishnah 1)
  - `m. Avot 1:1` (Pirkei Avot chapter 1, mishnah 1)
  - `m. Sanh. 4:5` (Mishnah Sanhedrin chapter 4, mishnah 5)

## 2. The Tosefta (תּוֹסֶפְתָּא)

Compiled in the 3rd century CE (traditionally attributed to Rabbi Chiya and Rabbi Oshaiah), the Tosefta ("Addition" / "Supplement") serves as a companion collection of Tannaitic traditions parallel to the Mishnah.
- Organized according to the same 6 orders as the Mishnah.
- Provides crucial variants, explanations, and lengthier traditions of Mishnaic laws.
- **Citation Format**: `t. [Tractate] [Chapter]:[Halakhah]` (e.g., `t. Ber. 1:1`).

## 3. Talmud Bavli (Babylonian Talmud - תַּלְמוּד בַּבְלִי)

Completed around **500–600 CE** in the academies of Sura, Pumbedita, and Nehardea in Babylonia under the leadership of **Rav Ashi** and **Ravina**. Sages of the Talmudic period are called **Amoraim** (explainers/speakers).

### Key Characteristics
- Written in a mixture of **Mishnaic Hebrew** and **Babylonian Jewish Aramaic**.
- Covers **37 tractates** of the Mishnah:
  - In *Zera'im*, only tractate *Berakhot* has Bavli Gemara (since agricultural laws applied principally in the Land of Israel).
  - All tractates in *Mo'ed*, *Nashim*, and *Nezikin* (except *Eduyot* and *Avot*, which consist of ethical adages and testimonies rather than halakhic debate).
  - Most of *Kodashim* (Temple laws).
  - In *Tahorot*, only tractate *Niddah* has Bavli Gemara.

### The Vilna Shas Page Layout & The Daf 2a Rule
The standard printed edition of the Babylonian Talmud was published by the **Romm Publishing House in Vilna** (1880–1886):
- **Page Layout**: The central column contains the **Mishnah** followed by the **Gemara**. The inner margin contains the commentary of **Rashi** (essential line-by-line guide). The outer margin contains the dialectical critiques of the **Tosafot** (French and German scholars of the 12th–13th centuries). The outer margins also contain cross-referencing apparatuses: *Ein Mishpat / Ner Mitzvah* (references to Maimonides and the Shulchan Aruch), *Torah Or* (scriptural references), and *Mesoret HaShas* (parallel Talmudic passages).
- **Pagination**: Cites are made by tractate name, folio number (*Daf*), and folio side (*Amud*):
  - Side **a** = recto (first side of the leaf)
  - Side **b** = verso (second side of the leaf)
- **The Invariant**: Every tractate in the Babylonian Talmud begins on **Daf 2a** (page 1 is reserved for the title page and front matter). **Any citation citing "Daf 1a" or "Daf 1b" is fictitious**.

#### Citation Format
- Academic standard (SBL): `b. [Tractate] [Daf][Side]`
- Examples:
  - `b. Ber. 2a` (Babylonian Talmud Berakhot folio 2a — the opening page of the Talmud)
  - `b. Šabb. 31a` (Babylonian Talmud Shabbat folio 31a — Hillel and the convert)
  - `b. Sanh. 37a` (Babylonian Talmud Sanhedrin folio 37a)

## 4. Talmud Yerushalmi (Jerusalem Talmud - תַּלְמוּד יְרוּשַׁלְמִי)

Also called the Palestinian Talmud or *Talmud de-Eretz Yisrael*. Redacted in the Land of Israel (Tiberias, Sepphoris, Caesarea) around **350–400 CE**, prior to the completion of the Bavli, under Roman persecution.

### Key Characteristics
- Written in **Galilean Jewish Aramaic** and Mishnaic Hebrew.
- Covers **39 tractates**: includes extensive Gemara on the agricultural laws of *Zera'im*, but lacks Gemara on *Kodashim* and *Tahorot* (except Niddah).
- Style is more concise, less heavily redacted, and preserves different traditions and legal rulings than the Bavli.
- In traditional Jewish jurisprudence, when the Bavli and Yerushalmi disagree, the **Talmud Bavli takes halakhic precedence** because it was redacted later.

#### Citation Formats
- Academic / Chapter-Halakhah: `y. [Tractate] [Chapter]:[Halakhah]` (e.g., `y. Ber. 1:1`).
- Edition Folio / Column: Referenced by the 1523 Venice Bomberg or Krotoshin edition folio and column (*a, b, c, d*), e.g., `y. Mak. 2:31d`.

## 5. Midrashic Literature (מִדְרָשׁ)

Midrash comprises biblical interpretation, verse exposition, and narrative expansion. It is fundamentally divided into:

### 1. Midrash Halakhah (Legal Midrashim)
Tannaitic works deriving legal rulings directly from biblical syntax:
- **Mekhilta de-Rabbi Yishmael** & **Mekhilta de-Rabbi Shimon bar Yochai**: On the book of Exodus (*Shemot*).
- **Sifra** (also called *Torat Kohanim*): On the book of Leviticus (*Vayikra*).
- **Sifrei**: On Numbers (*Bamidbar*) and Deuteronomy (*Devarim*).

### 2. Midrash Aggadah (Narrative & Homiletical Midrashim)
Amoraic and post-Talmudic collections of theological, ethical, and narrative expositions:
- **Midrash Rabbah**: The largest collection, spanning Genesis Rabbah (*Bereshit Rabbah*, 5th c.), Leviticus Rabbah (*Vayikra Rabbah*), and the Five Megillot.
- **Midrash Tanchuma** (Yelammedenu): Homiletic midrash on the Pentateuch based on the triennial reading cycle.
- **Pirkei de-Rabbi Eliezer**: 8th-century narrative midrash covering Creation through the wilderness wanderings.
- **Yalkut Shimoni**: 13th-century anthology compiling midrashim across the entire Tanakh.

#### Citation Format
- Academic standard: `[Midrash Name] [Chapter/Section]:[Paragraph]`
- Examples: `Gen. Rab. 1:1`, `Mek. Bahodesh 1`, `Sifra Vayikra 1:1`.
